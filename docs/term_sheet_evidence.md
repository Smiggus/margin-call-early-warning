# Term sheet evidence: margin loans and equity TRS

Real margin loan and equity swap terms from public SEC EDGAR exhibits (plus the Credit Suisse / Paul Weiss Archegos report filed on EDGAR), used to calibrate the loan and TRS simulation. The wider set of real deals and outcomes is in `deal_universe.md`.

Labels used below:
- **[doc]**: the filed document states it, with the clause quoted or named.
- **[inf]**: inference or calculation. Implied LTVs are loan amount / (pledged shares x closing price on the funding date).
- **[red]**: the term exists in the document, but the number is redacted or sits in an unfiled side letter ("Additional Terms Letter", "Ratio Letter", "Fee and Ratio Letter", "Schedule 1.1C").

Since about 2015, US margin loan exhibits almost always keep the numeric ladder (initial, call, reset and release levels, cure time and spread) in a confidential side letter. The structure is visible in the filings, but the levels mostly are not. The numbers here come from documents that disclose them (Musk, Ping An, MHR/JPM, swap confirmations) and from implied LTVs.

## Margin loans vs TRS: structural comparison

### 1. Can TRS collateral calls use the same ladder logic?
Yes, if the TRS is restated as a loan. **[inf]** A long TRS client has economically borrowed the notional N0 against n shares the dealer holds as a hedge. Collateral is IA plus cumulative VM, and VM is roughly N0 minus the current value S·n. So the dealer's uncovered financing equals S·n minus IA. That gives an exposure ratio comparable to LTV:

`ER_TRS = (S·n - IA) / (S·n) = 1 - IA / MV`

The four ladder levels then map as follows:
- **Opening level** = 1 - IA%. Examples: 85% for Commerzbank/OTE, 92.5% for DB/Vornado, 80% for CS/Archegos before 2019, 0% for the fully collateralised Citi/Glencore and Lehman/Quest trades.
- **Call level**: under a zero-threshold CSA, any move bigger than the MTA. That is 0.25m USD at Nomura and min($10m, 2.5% of notional) at DB. So the call level sits a hair above the opening level, not 10 to 20 points above it as in a margin loan.
- **Reset level** = the opening level. The CSA Delivery Amount restores exposure fully, which matches Ping An's "less than or equal to the Initial LTV Ratio" cure.
- **Enforcement**: failure to transfer becomes an Event of Default after the ISDA grace period. **[inf]** That grace is typically 1 to 2 Local Business Days under the ISDA/CSA, not stated in these confirmations.

So one ladder engine can serve both products. For a TRS, set `call = open + MTA/MV` and `reset = open`, and run it daily.

### 2. Where they genuinely differ
1. **Buffer and call frequency.** Margin loans leave a wide band between opening and call. The Musk ladder was 20/25/35 (a [doc] example), and implied openings here are 19 to 47% [inf]. Calls in a margin loan are therefore rare and large. TRS VM calls are daily and small, with no band. For an early-warning model, the TRS signal is the size of the VM call relative to the client's liquidity, and IA erosion. It is not the distance to a call level.
2. **Static vs re-marked IA.** This is the single most important TRS design choice for the simulation.
   - Static-dollar IA on a bullet swap (Archegos [doc]; Glencore is also static but at 100%): IA as a % of current MV erodes when the price rises. Leverage builds silently on the way up.
   - Re-marked IA: Nomura recomputes IA daily at IA% x the current close [doc]. Commerzbank's IA follows the monthly-reset ENA [doc]. Either way the IA% stays constant against market value, which behaves like a margin loan with constant LTV.
   - The equity notional reset is the mechanism that converts the first type into the second. Evidence: monthly (Commerzbank/OTE), weekly (VimpelCom/ING), and Not Applicable (Nomura, Citi, CoreLogic, Archegos bullets).
3. **Collateral composition and wrong-way risk.** In a margin loan the collateral is the shares themselves. LTV moves one-for-one with price, and liquidation risk drives the haircuts: disrupted-day price discounts of 5% per day at MS [doc], and FAE discounts capped at 25% at Citi [doc]. TRS collateral is cash or treasuries at 97 to 100% [doc]. The dealer's hedge is the share, so liquidation risk shows up through the IA level and ADTV-linked add-ons, not through haircuts on the collateral.
4. **Trigger families.**
   - Margin loans add price-path triggers alongside LTV: price barriers (Verint and Zix "Reference/Share Price Trigger Threshold" [doc/red], Ping An 35% of base price [doc], MHR 45% of initial collateral value [doc]). They also add persistence rules (Ping An 3 consecutive days [doc], similar to Pirelli's 3 closes) and tiered cure by LTV (CDEV 1 BD above 65%, otherwise 2 BD [doc]).
   - TRS add counterparty-level ATEs: NAV floor USD 500m (Nomura [doc]), rating and CDS > 4.5% (DB [doc]), and dealer ownership above 9.9% (Citi [doc]). They also give the dealer an optional termination or spread re-set right (DB, Commerzbank [doc]).
   - The model should carry these as separate hazard features, not fold them into the LTV ladder.
5. **Cure time.**
   - Margin loans: same or next BD to 2 BD is standard (CDEV, ESH, MS, TransAlta [doc]). Extensions run to 3 BD for share top-ups (MHR) or 4 STD with evidence of steps taken (TransAlta). Ping An at 11 BD is the outlier, an Asian or LMA style bank facility.
   - TRS: T+0 or T+1 transfer of the delivery amount (Nomura [doc]), then the ISDA grace period [inf].
6. **Release.** Margin loans release excess only below a release level, sometimes with persistence (Ping An: 10 consecutive days below Initial LTV [doc]). TRS return excess VM on demand. The Archegos report shows the danger: the client "began calling the excess variation margin" [doc].
7. **Pricing.**
   - Margin loans are wider: LIBOR + 1.00% (Ping An), + 2.00% plus a 0.94% undrawn fee (JPM/MHR), SOFR + 3% (Musk), plus structuring or upfront fees and make-wholes. Verint's make-whole is 50% of the margin x remaining days.
   - TRS are tighter: EURIBOR + 0.10 to 0.125% (Commerzbank 2007), LIBOR + 0.80 to 0.95% (DB 2012), E + 1.43% (Pirelli 2002) and L + 1.20 to 1.25% (Seabridge), OBFR + spread [red] (Nomura).
   - [inf] The gap reflects IA posted in cash and the dealer's hedge ownership.

**Bottom line [inf]:** The hypothesis holds in a qualified form. Both products reduce to an exposure ratio checked daily against open / call / reset / enforcement levels, so one ladder engine works. They differ in three parameters. TRS have a near-zero buffer between open and call, an IA that is either static or re-marked depending on the reset convention, and counterparty-level ATEs in place of price barriers. Calibrate the TRS book with 1 - IA% as the opening ER, MTA-sized calls, and an explicit reset-frequency flag. Bullet trades with static IA should be the high-risk subset, the Archegos pattern.
